# Question-invariant conventions control audit

Describe control of written conventions independently of topic, position,
quality of ideas, response length, or apparent effort. A fluent argument can
still contain recurring convention failures; an unambitious response can still
control the forms it attempts.

Read the whole response in three passes. First, read normally and note where
rhythm or meaning is interrupted. Second, inspect sentence boundaries, clause
connections, grammar, usage, punctuation, and orthography. Third, compare the
beginning, middle, and end to distinguish isolated lapses from recurring or
pervasive patterns.

Keep four observations separate:

- frequency: how often a verified problem occurs;
- dispersion: whether it is isolated, clustered, or recurring across sections;
- diversity: whether one copied form repeats or several independent systems fail;
- repair burden: whether the reader makes a local edit, rebuilds a clause or
  sentence, rereads repeatedly, or cannot recover the intended relationship.

Do not convert an automated flag count into a judgment. Verify each plausible
pattern against the response. Do not count repetitions of one misspelled word
as independent systems failing. Do not require advanced punctuation forms to
appear. Complexity is neither a reward nor a penalty: describe control of the
structures actually attempted.
