# CV field-scoped observations

Use only the section named for the requested field. Judge control from an ordinary first reading of the response, independent of content quality. Automated flags are fallible observations, not errors or scores. Repeated instances of one pattern show recurrence but not many distinct error types.

## I
Inspect whether meaning is recovered continuously, with brief local distraction, with repeated rereading, or only after reconstruction. Ignore automated error counts.

## R
Inspect the scope of mental repair needed to preserve intended meaning: none, a local edit, reconstruction of clauses, or reconstruction of the message. Ignore automated error counts.

## G
Inspect grammatical relationships in context. Verify candidate problems against the intended sentence; do not treat stylistic preferences as grammatical failure.

## S
Inspect sentence boundaries and syntactic completeness. Distinguish controlled complexity from a long sentence whose relationships become unstable.

## U
Inspect word forms, agreement, reference, and idiomatic use where they affect control. Count a recurring pattern as recurring, not as many unrelated failures.

## P
Inspect whether punctuation reliably marks boundaries and relationships. Formatting conventions that do not affect reading carry less weight than ambiguous boundaries.

## O
Inspect spelling, capitalization, spacing, and related orthographic patterns. Verify names, dialect forms, and code-switching before treating a flag as evidence.

## A
Inspect the range of sentence structures the writer controls. Complexity attempted without control is not syntactic ambition successfully realized.

## C
Inspect how consistently control is maintained across the response. Isolated local slips differ from a recurring pattern or instability across several domains.
