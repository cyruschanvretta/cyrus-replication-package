# Generalized expert-rater distinctions

Expert scoring language repeatedly separates merely naming a point from developing it.
A list of reasons can be relevant yet limited when the response does not explain causes,
consequences, examples, or significance. Conversely, plain wording can support a high
Topic Development score when details are specific, connected, and deliberately ordered.

For the middle Topic Development bands, ask whether the details are sufficient as a
set, not whether any single detail is impressive. For the upper bands, ask whether
specificity is sustained and whether the sequence of ideas adds meaning rather than
following a mechanical template. Factual-sounding names or statistics count only for
the reasoning work they perform.

For Conventions, experts judge the reader's experience. Distinguish a response that is
decodable only through repeated repair, a response whose recurring errors remain
noticeable, a response with errors that recede behind the message, and a response with
consistent control. Complexity is not required for control, and ambitious syntax is not
evidence of control when it repeatedly breaks down.

For Open Response, a correct claim without relevant support is different from a claim
whose support is selected and explained. Apply the authored rubric's exact distinction.
