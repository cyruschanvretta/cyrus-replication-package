# Question-invariant conventions burden audit

Describe control of written conventions independently of the topic, position,
quality of ideas, or apparent effort. A fluent argument can still contain recurring
convention failures; an unambitious response can still control the forms it attempts.

Read the whole response in three passes. First, read normally and note where rhythm or
meaning is interrupted. Second, inspect sentence boundaries, clause connections,
grammar, usage, punctuation, and orthography. Third, compare the beginning, middle,
and end to distinguish isolated lapses from a pattern that recurs across the response.

Keep four observations separate:

- frequency: how often a verified problem occurs;
- dispersion: whether it is isolated, clustered, or recurring across sections;
- diversity: whether one copied form repeats or several independent systems fail;
- repair burden: whether the reader makes a local edit, rebuilds a clause or sentence,
  rereads repeatedly, or cannot recover the intended relationship.

Do not convert an automated flag count into a judgment. Verify each plausible pattern
against the response. Do not count the same misspelled word repeatedly as independent
evidence. Do not require every advanced punctuation form to appear. Complexity is
neither a reward nor a penalty: describe whether the structures actually attempted are
controlled.

Use the full burden scale. Zero means no credible burden or only isolated harmless
lapses. One means several local lapses that remain easy to repair. Two requires a
recurring pattern that distracts or forces repeated repair. Three requires pervasive
failure or repeatedly blocked meaning. Do not choose an extreme merely because a
response is long, short, polished in ideas, or visibly errorful.
