# Boundary ladder

Evaluate adjacent boundaries explicitly rather than selecting a score from a global
impression.

For Topic Development, record separate findings for opinion clarity/consistency,
number and sufficiency of distinct reasons, explanation of how details support those
reasons, specificity across the response, and organizational progression. A named
example is not automatically specific: specificity must make a reason clearer, more
concrete, or more persuasive. A transition is not automatically organization: later
ideas must have a discernible relationship to earlier ones.

For Conventions, distinguish interference, distraction, non-distraction, and control
by the reading work imposed. Ask whether meaning is lost, whether rereading is frequent,
whether errors recur across several convention categories, and whether well-formed
sentences predominate. Isolated errors are compatible with control; understandable text
can still be distracting when interruptions are persistent.

For Open Response, use only the adjacent distinctions in the authored item rubric.
Separate answer accuracy, relevant evidence, and explanation before mapping to a band.
