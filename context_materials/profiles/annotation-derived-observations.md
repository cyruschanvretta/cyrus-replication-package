# General observations distilled from scorer annotations

These question-invariant observations were distilled from authored bilingual scorer
guides and response-set annotations. No level-2 labels or responses were used.

- Breadth and specificity are independent: several generic reasons differ from one
  concrete reason, and neither alone establishes depth.
- A detail can be concrete yet weakly linked. Judge whether the writer makes the
  support-to-position connection explicit or leaves it for the reader.
- Explanation can merely restate an example, explain how or why it matters, or extend
  to consequences and significance.
- Mechanical ordering and visible transition words do not guarantee progression.
  Distinguish list-like sequence from ideas that build on one another.
- Repetition can preserve focus while failing to advance it. Track both consistency
  and advancement rather than treating them as one feature.
- Functional openings, development, and closures may occur without conventional
  paragraph formatting.
- For conventions, frequency, dispersion, and repair scope are different. Many copies
  of one local error are not equivalent to varied errors across sentence boundaries.
- Sentence ambition changes the evidence needed for control. Do not reward complexity
  by itself or punish a controlled simple sentence merely for being simple.
- The central impact question is reader work: no interruption, brief distraction,
  repeated rereading, or blocked meaning. Error counts inform this description but do
  not determine it.
