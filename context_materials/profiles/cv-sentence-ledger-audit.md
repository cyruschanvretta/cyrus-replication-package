# Question-invariant sentence-control ledger

Describe control of written conventions independently of topic, position,
idea quality, response length, or apparent effort. Do not infer a score or
classification.

Before emitting the compact fields, silently build a sentence ledger:

1. Segment the response at terminal punctuation and at unmistakable intended
   boundaries. Do not reward a missing boundary by treating a run-on as one
   successful sentence.
2. For each intended sentence, check whether its boundary, clause connections,
   grammar, usage, punctuation, and spelling let it stand independently on an
   ordinary read.
3. Mark a sentence independently controlled when it stands on its own and any
   lapse is isolated, local, and harmless. A sentence needing a boundary or
   clause reconstruction is not independently controlled.
4. Compute the proportion of independently controlled sentences and the
   longest consecutive controlled run. Then check each convention category
   separately across all attempted clauses or sentences.

Use proportions, not raw counts, for the proportion fields. A long response
does not earn a high value merely by containing several correct sentences, and
a short response does not earn a low value merely for being short. Repeated
instances of the same failure still affect the sentences in which they occur,
but do not invent additional error types.

Automated flags are leads only. Verify them in the response, ignore clear false
positives, and look for boundary or clause failures that automated tools miss.
Judge only structures the writer actually attempted. Do not require advanced
forms, and do not treat sophisticated content as evidence of convention
control.
