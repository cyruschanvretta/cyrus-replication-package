# TD field-scoped observations

Use only the section named for the requested field. Describe what is visibly present in the writing. Relevance, length, fluency, or a conventional essay shape must not raise an unrelated field. Strongest-state evidence must be sustained, not merely attempted once.

## T
Inspect whether the writing addresses the task itself. Separate a direct answer from copied wording, topic association, or a response that shifts to a different issue.

## P
Inspect whether a position or controlling focus can be identified and whether it remains stable. Do not reward breadth, examples, or polish here.

## B
Inventory independent support units. Rewording the same reason, adding parallel details to it, or repeating a claim does not create another unit.

## S
Inspect the usable precision inside support units. A name, number, or scenario is concrete only when it clarifies the support rather than decorating it.

## L
Inspect the inferential link from each support unit to the position. Mere adjacency, topic similarity, or a connective word is not an explained link.

## E
Inventory support operations: naming, illustrating, explaining how or why, extending a consequence, and establishing significance. Count operations only when their relationship is intelligible.

## O
Inspect relationships among parts: sequence, grouping, contrast, cause, qualification, or conclusion. Paragraphing and transition words alone do not establish progression.

## A
Inspect whether later material changes or deepens what the reader understands. Parallel additions and restatement do not count as advancement.

## F
Inspect whether the response performs the functional roles it attempts: establishing focus, developing it, and bringing it to a usable close. Do not equate formulaic headings with completed roles.

## C
Inspect whether claims and support remain compatible. Qualification can strengthen consistency; unresolved contradiction or drift weakens it.
